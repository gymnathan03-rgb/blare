Privacy Policy — Blare Alarm Clock

Last updated: September 2026

Blare ("the app") is an alarm clock web app. This page explains what data the app accesses and how it's used.

Data stored locally on your device

Your alarms, settings, and streak history are stored only in your browser's local storage on your own device. This data is never sent to or stored on any server we operate.

Balance Challenge (camera)

If you enable the Balance Challenge dismiss method, the app accesses your device's camera to detect whether an object is balanced above your head. This detection runs entirely on-device using on-device machine learning models — no image, video, or camera data is ever uploaded, transmitted, or stored anywhere. Camera access stops the moment the challenge ends.

Wake-Up Playlist (YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music)

If you choose to connect a music account to use as your wake-up sound, the app requests read-only access to list your playlists from that service (for example, the youtube.readonly scope for YouTube). This is used only to let you pick a playlist to wake up to, and to play it back through that service's official player at alarm time. We do not store your playlists, listening history, or any other data from these services on our own servers — the connection and any access tokens are held only in your browser.

You can revoke this access at any time through your Google, Spotify, or Apple account security settings.

Wake-Up Stakes (Stripe)

If you subscribe to put money on waking up, payment is processed by Stripe. We do not store your card details — Stripe handles that directly. Our backend stores only what's needed to track your subscription status and any active stake hold (a Stripe customer ID and payment intent IDs), tied to a random identifier generated for your device, not your name or email.

Analytics

We do not run any third-party analytics or tracking on this app.
